# Episode 1, script v3: cold listener notes

*I heard the narration once, at about 150 words a minute. I didn't read the drawing cues or the runtime table, so the pictures may cover a few small things (like "colonnades"). I read the Cold listener checklist only after the listen. I listened as a working parent who goes to church and isn't a theologian.*

## The short version

The coin story at the start grips me, and the payoff in section 7 is worth waiting for. "You are the statue that breathes, and the coin that walks" is the line I'd repeat at dinner.

The weak stretch is the middle. Section 2 asks "what belonged to God?" After that, Jesus is offstage from the Galilee quote in section 3 until "Now hear Jesus' answer again" in section 7. Meanwhile I'm taught what a kingdom is three times: the Lord's Prayer, the boundary stone, and the farmer. The farmer works. The boundary stone doesn't.

The farmer's king takes the grain and the sons. That made God feel harsh, and the story never quite settles it.

The ending gave me one real thing to do: pray "Your kingdom come" over the part of my life I've kept back. But the cash habit doesn't fit how I pay, and the outro weakens the ending.

## 1. What I felt in each section

- **Opening (channel intro):** Waiting. A web address before anything has happened. I'd have reached for "skip".
- **1. The coin:** Hooked from "It was a Tuesday". Real tension. I know this story, but the detail made it new. The soldiers watching from the colonnades made the trap feel dangerous, not just clever. The best three minutes of the episode.
- **2. What belongs to God?:** Curious, then a small "ah, here comes the lesson." The narrator steps in with "I believe", and the story turns into a thesis.
- **3. A word that lost its weight:** I recognised myself in the castle and the dragon, and in rushing the Lord's Prayer "on the way to daily bread", which made me smile. One genuine "oh!" at "They ask for the same thing twice." It felt a bit scattered: Galilee, a storybook castle, Sunday church, then back to the temple courts, all in about a minute.
- **4. The edge of a kingdom:** Patient but drifting. It felt like the same definition again, this time from a museum guide. The traveller has no name and nothing at stake.
- **5. The farmer who forgot his king:** Drawn in. The father buried in the field got me. Then I got uneasy: the king takes the grain, there's no arguing, and the sons are called away to his wall. Relief when the enemy army turns it around. "He could forget the king only because the king was doing his job" hit hard. Then I felt convicted ("Most of us live a lot like that farmer"), then sat through a civics lesson, then smiled at "nobody ever voted God in."
- **6. The king's face:** "He sent his face" hooked me. The Cyprus stone felt like a plaque in a museum. "Remember that coin in the temple courts?" was a relief: the detour had been going somewhere after all.
- **7. The first page:** The click. Everything snapped together: the farmer, the stone face, the coin. Goosebumps at "the statue that breathes, and the coin that walks." Then the weight of "The ownership question was total."
- **8. What the image gives you:** Moved. The grandmother who no longer remembers her own name hit me harder than anything else in the episode. Then the responsibility part went by before I'd caught up.
- **9. The question comes home:** A tender bookend. The men walking home was quiet in the best way. I actually looked at my hand. Then came a practical step, and half of it doesn't fit my life.
- **10. Next time:** Polite interest. It sounds like homework.
- **Closing (channel outro):** I lost the thread: a comment request, a web address, someone called Carl, and a slogan I didn't understand.

## 2. The moment I leaned in most

Section 7. It runs from "And then, last of all, He does something every person in that ancient world would have understood." through "He places His image in His land." to "Caesar could stamp his face on silver. God had stamped His on them."

That's where the long detour paid off all at once. I sat forward because I could feel the answer coming before it was said. When it came, it was bigger than I'd guessed.

Close behind:
- "They ask for the same thing twice." (the best small "aha")
- "He sent his face." (the best hook line)
- "So does a grandmother who no longer remembers her own name" (the emotional high)

## 3. Where my attention drifted

1. **Channel intro.** Sentence before: "This is Understanding Kingdom, exploring the Kingdom of God through Scripture, covenant and the life of faith." Then "Visit us at www.afaithdrivenlife.com." A web address before the story is where a YouTube thumb goes to "skip". The strongest first line in the script, "It was a Tuesday, in the last week of Jesus' life.", waits behind it. I know this is the standard channel intro. *What to do:* get to the Tuesday sooner, or save the address for the outro.
2. **Start of section 4.** Sentence before: "For the people in those temple courts, that was simply how the world worked." Then "In the world of the Bible, a king's land had edges, and stones marked them." I'd just been told what a kingdom was, and now a nameless traveller tells me again, with Jesus and the coin nowhere in sight. This is where I'd have checked my phone. The farmer got me back. *What to do:* fold the stone into the farmer's story (he lives "inside that line" anyway), or add one line saying we're heading back to the coin.
3. **Section 5, the democracy paragraph.** Sentence before: "We take His patience to mean that He is absent." Four sentences of civics I already know ("We vote our leaders in, and we can vote them out."). A mild drift. "Nobody ever voted God in" pulled me back, and that line is the keeper. *What to do:* get to that line faster.
4. **Section 6, Cyprus.** Sentence before: "He set it up where the people of that land would pass it day after day." The Cyprus stone told me the same thing a second time. The king is "far across the sea" and "far away", right after "a far-off land" and "places he would never visit." A mild drift. "Remember that coin in the temple courts?" brought me back. *What to do:* one sentence of fact, not three.
5. **Section 7, creation.** Sentence before: "And God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light." "Day by day He fills His world..." is the most familiar part of the episode, so I coasted. It was brief: "He does something every person in that ancient world would have understood" snapped me straight back. *What to do:* keep it short. It nearly is already.
6. **Section 10.** Sentence before: "Your kingdom come. Your will be done." For me the story was over there. "The kind of document a great king used to bind a people to himself" sounds like a reading assignment. This is where I'd click the next video in my feed. *What to do:* tease the next episode with something I can see, the way I could see the coin.

The bigger pattern: section 2 asks the question, and by my feel about six minutes pass before section 7 answers it. The farmer earns his time. Sections 3, 4 and 6 are where I needed a reminder of where we were going.

## 4. What I didn't understand on one hearing

7. "On the other side, the coin called Tiberius Pontifex Maximus, the highest priest." For a second I heard "the coin called Tiberius" as the coin's name. *What to do:* use "named him" or similar so the ear doesn't trip.
8. "At festivals they came out and stood along the colonnades, watching the crowd." I don't use the word "colonnades". The drawing will have to carry it.
9. "One of them took a silver denarius from his own purse and put it in Jesus' hand." I think "his own purse" was a dig: the man who hates Rome carries Rome's money. I only half caught it. *What to do:* if it matters, let it land. If not, it does no harm.
10. "In one small piece of silver, a man claimed to be the son of a god, and the one who stood between his people and heaven." As a churchgoer, I hear those as Jesus' titles, and the real one was standing there holding the coin. I waited the whole episode for the script to say so, and it never did. *What to do:* pay it off once, or accept that churchgoers will be waiting for it.
11. "Listen to the first two lines again." My reflex was: the first line is "Our Father". *What to do:* say "those two lines".
12. "Now imagine a farmer who lives inside that line, in the ancient kingdom of Assyria." Why Assyria? I know the Assyrians as Israel's enemy, the people Jonah ran from. Holding up their king as the picture of God's kingship gave me a "wait, them?" moment, and nobody told me why we'd jumped there from Roman Jerusalem. *What to do:* add one clause on why this kingdom.
13. "There is no arguing with them. Nobody asks the farmer whether he agrees." and "The farmer's sons are called away to work on it, and they go."
    - As a parent, the strongest picture in section 5 was the sons leaving for the king's wall and the father bringing in the harvest alone. It made the king someone who takes your children.
    - The turn ("why he has paid and laboured all these years") answers that in one word. But the soldiers save the family, not the wall, so the sons' loss never quite comes back around.
    - So when I heard "Most of us live a lot like that farmer," I put God in that king's place and flinched.
    - "His field was held under the king" also briefly sounded like "held down by the king."

    *What to do:* let the wall the sons built be part of what saves the family, or say plainly that the picture is about who owns the field, not about how gentle the king was.
14. "We take His patience to mean that He is absent." The farmer's story was about protection ("the king was doing his job"), not patience. "Patience" felt like it came from a different story. *What to do:* use the word the story earned.
15. **Two kinds of stones.** In section 4 the stones carry "the names of the kings". In section 6 they carry the king's face. On one hearing I couldn't tell whether they were the same stones, and section 6 felt like "didn't we already do stones?" *What to do:* make them one stone, or make the difference obvious.
16. "...so that they may rule..." The quote breaks off on "rule" and never comes back to it. I was asking "rule what?" and missed the next line. *What to do:* end the quote before "so that they may rule", or pick up "rule" later.
17. "And to God what is God's, the person who carries His." Out loud, "His" sounds like "his", and "his" had just meant Caesar ("the coin with his face on it"). It took me a beat to work out that it meant God's face. *What to do:* say "His face" or "His image".
18. "The King's image also lays a responsibility on you." and "That goes for the image you carry, and for the same image on every face you meet." This went by too fast after the grandmother. I couldn't tell you what "the image you carry" asks of me. The worth half had three pictures (the baby, the grandmother, the prisoner), and the responsibility half has none. *What to do:* give it one picture I can see.
19. "Hold out your hand for a moment, and look at it. Whose image is this?" It worked, and I did it. But my hand doesn't have a face on it, and only afterwards did I connect it to "the palm of a hand" from near the start. *What to do:* a light nod back to the coin lying in Jesus' palm would make the gesture click.
20. **Outro.** "Remember, Jesus plus nothing." I don't know what that means here. After twelve minutes about giving God every part of me, "plus nothing" sounded like it pulled the other way. I also don't know who Carl is ("send Carl a message"). And I wasn't sure whether the show is called "A Faith Driven Life" or "Understanding Kingdom". I know these are the standard intro and outro, but they still cost the ending something.

## 5. What I'll remember tomorrow

- Kings stamped their face on stones and coins to say "this is mine." God stamped His face on people. Caesar can have the coin; God gets me, all of me, every day.
- "You're the statue that breathes and the coin that walks."
- "Your kingdom come" and "your will be done" are the same prayer said twice. A kingdom is where the king gets his way.
- The farmer who forgot the king because the king was keeping him safe. Nobody voted God in.
- A grandmother who has forgotten her own name still carries God's image in full.
- Pray "your kingdom come" over the part of my life I've kept back.

What I won't remember: Tiberius, Augustus, Pontifex Maximus, the Herodians, Cyprus, the boundary stone, the democracy bit, or the next episode's title. I'd say "something about a treaty", and Assyria will become "some ancient empire".

## 6. Did the ending make me want to do something?

Yes, more than most church videos do.

- **In the moment:** I'd actually hold out my hand and look at it.
- **This week:** I'd most likely pray "Your kingdom come, your will be done" over one specific thing. "The one part of your life you have been keeping for yourself" brought something to mind straight away. For me it was my evenings.
- **Next Sunday:** I'd say the Lord's Prayer more slowly.

What got in the way:
- **The cash habit.** "Every time money passes through your hand, let the face on it ask you that question." I pay with a card or my phone, so money almost never passes through my hand and the habit would never kick in. Also, "Here is one thing to do this week" turned into two things.
- **What comes after the prayer.** A teaser, a comment request, a web address and a slogan: four new things to think about, right after the one thing I was meant to take away.

I'd watch episode 2 because of this episode, not because of the teaser.

## 7. Audiobook, sermon or lecture?

Overall, a sermon built on a story. It opens as an audiobook, becomes a guided lecture in the middle, and lands as a sermon, a good one. I drifted in the lecture parts. I leaned in where story and sermon touched: "He could forget the king only because the king was doing his job" and "Caesar could stamp his face on silver. God had stamped His on them."

Where it changes:
- **Audiobook:** from "It was a Tuesday, in the last week of Jesus' life." to "They left Him and went away."
- **The narrator steps in and the lecture starts:** "The men who heard Him knew the answer, and I believe that is what amazed them.", then the thesis, "They knew what a kingdom actually was."
- **Friendly lecture:** section 3.
- **Cold lecture:** section 4, from "In the world of the Bible, a king's land had edges, and stones marked them."
- **Audiobook again:** from "Now imagine a farmer who lives inside that line" to "He could forget the king only because the king was doing his job."
- **Sermon:** "Most of us live a lot like that farmer."
- **Lecture:** "Part of the reason we forget Him is the world we grew up in."
- **Sermon:** "That includes the farmer, and it includes you."
- **Documentary:** "One of those stones was found on the island of Cyprus"
- **Back to the story:** "Remember that coin in the temple courts?"
- **Bible story turning into sermon:** section 7, from "Now go back to the first page of the Bible" to "So whose face do you carry?" From there it's sermon through the end of section 8.
- **Audiobook:** "I often think about those men walking home that evening."
- **Altar call:** "And now that question has come all the way to you."
- **Announcement:** "Next time, we will open the kind of document a great king used to bind a people to himself"
