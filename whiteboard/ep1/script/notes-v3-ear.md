# Ear editor notes: Episode 1, script v3

*Ear editor pass on `ep1-script-v3.md`. I read every narration sentence aloud against the "Ear editor" checklist (`editors.md`) and craft.md section 9. Drawing cues are ignored. Notes follow script order. Each one quotes the sentence, names the problem and gives a replacement line in the author's voice.*

**What is already clean:** no dash asides, no parentheses, none of the banned words (profound, tapestry, delve, journey, realm…), and no "not X but Y" frames. Most sentences fit in one breath.

**What repeats across the whole script** (fixes are in the numbered notes where each first appears):

- "Most of us" opens or carries four sentences (sections 2, 3, 5, 5), and "Many of us" a fifth. On the ear it becomes a verbal tic.
- "Now" opens five sentences. Three of them are hinges ("Now imagine", "Now go back", "Now hear").
- One idea has three words: face, image and likeness. The refrain is "Whose image is this?", so the narration should say *image* (and *face* where the drawing says it) and drop *likeness* outside the Genesis quote.
- Pronouns point nowhere at three key turns: "that is what amazed them", "It means you", "This is where your worth comes from".
- From section 7 on, the script lands on a neat contrast couplet almost every 20 seconds (silver/them, stone/alive, small/total, coin/themselves). Each is fine alone. Together they start to sound machine-made.
- Capital letters are invisible on a voice track. "His", "He" and "the King" only work where the sentence itself makes clear it means God.

## Opening (channel intro)

**1.** "Visit us at www.afaithdrivenlife.com."
*Problem:* written for the eye. "Double-u double-u double-u dot" is a mouthful, and the outro already says the address without it. The listener also never hears the episode title, which is the audiobook's chapter heading.
*Fix:* "Visit us at afaithdrivenlife.com. Episode one: What a Kingdom Actually Was." (The intro is fixed channel copy, so this is optional. It answers story-bible question 3.)

## 1. The coin

**2.** "Jerusalem was full for the Passover, the feast that remembered how God had brought His people out of slavery in Egypt."
*Problem:* it sounds like an article. The listener gets a footnote hung off an appositive, three clauses deep. People remember; a feast does not.
*Fix:* "Jerusalem was packed for the Passover. The whole city was remembering how God had brought His people out of slavery in Egypt."

**3.** "The men made their way to the front."
*Problem:* unclear reference. The men were last mentioned two sentences ago, and "people had gathered round" sits in between. On one hearing, "the men" could be the listeners.
*Fix:* "The men with the question pushed their way to the front."

**4.** "His armies had conquered this land, and his soldiers kept watch over Jerusalem from a fortress at the corner of the temple courts." / "At festivals they came out and stood along the colonnades, watching the crowd."
*Problem:* accidental repetition. "Kept watch", "watching the crowd" and then "those soldiers were watching" give three *watch* words in half a minute, which spends the payoff before it arrives. "From a fortress at the corner of the temple courts" also stacks three prepositions.
*Fix:* "His armies had conquered this land, and his soldiers held a fortress at the corner of the temple courts." Keep "watching the crowd" and "those soldiers were watching".

**5.** "So everyone listening could see the trap."
*Problem:* on a voice track, "everyone listening" sounds like the audience.
*Fix:* "So everyone in that crowd could see the trap."

**6.** "One of them took a silver denarius from his own purse and put it in Jesus' hand."
*Problem:* the word *coin* is not spoken until a09, after the listener has already heard "On one side was a man's face". A listener who does not know "denarius" wonders, one side of what? "Denarius" is said twice in two sentences, and "hand" is said here and again in the very next line, which blunts the author's punchline.
*Fix:* "One of them took a silver coin from his own purse and gave it to Jesus." Then "The whole trap was small enough to fit in the palm of a hand." owns the word *hand*.

**7.** "On one side was a man's face, the emperor Tiberius."
*Problem:* two names for one man. The listener has learned "Caesar" and now hears "Tiberius", and may think there are two people. "Tiberius" is said four times in 20 seconds.
*Fix:* "On one side was the face of Caesar himself, the emperor Tiberius."

**8.** "On the other side, the coin called Tiberius Pontifex Maximus, the highest priest."
*Problem:* by ear, "the coin called Tiberius" sounds like the coin's name. It is also the fourth Tiberius.
*Fix:* "On the other side was a second title. Pontifex Maximus, the highest priest." This matches "Around the face ran a claim." Apply it together with note 6, so the word *coin* is still heard in this section.

**9.** "In one small piece of silver, a man claimed to be the son of a god, and the one who stood between his people and heaven."
*Problem:* breath and structure (26 words). The listener has to hold "claimed to be" across the comma to make sense of "and the one who stood". The subject arrives late.
*Fix:* "That small piece of silver made two claims. This man was the son of a god, and he stood between his people and heaven."

## 2. What belongs to God?

**10.** "If the coin with Caesar's face belonged to Caesar, what belonged to God?"
*Problem:* none. Keep it. It is the dramatic question, though, and the listener has to carry it for six minutes without seeing a heading. The author's own habit is to say so.
*Fix (add after it):* "I want you to hold onto that question." (His line: "I want you to hold onto that phrase for just a moment.")

**11.** "The men who heard Him knew the answer, and I believe that is what amazed them."
*Problem:* unclear pronoun. "That" could mean the answer, or the fact that they knew it.
*Fix:* "The men who heard Him knew the answer straight away. I believe that is why they were amazed."

**12.** "Most of us miss it, because we have forgotten what they knew."
*Problem:* this is the first of four "most of us" (also "For most of us, the word has gone soft", "Most of us live a lot like that farmer", "Most of us live in democracies"). Keep this one and vary the rest (see notes 13, 21 and 23).
*Fix:* keep as written.

## 3. A word that lost its weight

**13.** "When Jesus first began to preach in Galilee, this is what He said."
*Problem:* the jump back in time is inaudible. The listener is in the temple on Tuesday and is suddenly years earlier in Galilee. "First began" says the same thing twice.
*Fix:* "Years before that Tuesday, when Jesus began to preach in Galilee, this is what He said." In the next paragraph, "For most of us, the word has gone soft" can become "Today the word has gone soft."

## 4. The edge of a kingdom

**14.** "Some of those stones survive today, still carrying the names of the kings who set them up."
*Problem:* it sounds like an article or a museum caption, and the participle tail slows the walk into the scene. "Still" comes back in section 6 ("It still carries…").
*Fix:* "A few of those stones have survived, with their kings' names still on them." Or cut the line, so the traveller arrives sooner.

**15.** "From here on, this king's law runs." / "His officials collect the taxes here, and his soldiers keep the peace." / "His protection covers everyone on this side of the line."
*Problem:* "law runs" is a written legal idiom, and by ear it sounds odd. The three sentences make a four-item list (law, taxes, peace, protection) in matching "His…" shapes, and "here" is said twice.
*Fix:* "From here on, this king's law holds. His officials collect the taxes, and his soldiers protect everyone on this side of the line." Keep "One step past that stone, and the traveller is under a different king."

## 5. The farmer who forgot his king

**16.** "Now imagine a farmer who lives inside that line, in the ancient kingdom of Assyria."
*Problem:* "Now" works as a hinge here, and again in "Now go back to the first page" and "Now hear Jesus' answer again". "Imagine" announces the scene instead of starting it, which is the same fault as v2's "let's step into their world".
*Fix:* "Take a farmer who lives inside a line like that, in the ancient kingdom of Assyria."

**17.** "He sells his grain and raises his sons."
*Problem:* rhythm. Seven sentences in a row run 6 to 14 words each, and four of them open with "He" or "His", so the montage starts to sound like a list.
*Fix:* join it to the next line: "He sells his grain and raises his sons, and when his father dies, he buries him in that ground."

**18.** "There is no arguing with them."
*Problem:* it says the same thing as the next sentence, "Nobody asks the farmer whether he agrees", which is the better line.
*Fix:* cut it.

**19.** "Now the farmer understands why he has paid and laboured all these years."
*Problem:* the line tells before it shows. The listener gets the understanding before the soldiers.
*Fix:* swap the order: "The king's soldiers are all that stand between that army and his family. Now the farmer understands what all those years of grain and labour were for."

**20.** "His field was held under the king the whole time."
*Problem:* written for the eye. "Held under" is a land-law phrase, and in the passive voice it can sound like "held down".
*Fix:* "All along, he had held that field under the king." This keeps the drawing's words and puts a person at the front of the sentence.

**21.** "Most of us live a lot like that farmer." / "We get up, go to work, raise our children and run our own lives."
*Problem:* this is the third "most of us", and the list has four items.
*Fix:* "We live a lot like that farmer. We go to work, raise our children and run our own lives."

**22.** "We take His patience to mean that He is absent."
*Problem:* an AI-style aphorism, and off-voice. "Take X to mean Y" is essay syntax. "Patience" is a new idea the farmer scene never set up, so on one hearing it does not connect. The farmer forgot his king because the king was doing his job, and that is the parallel the listener is waiting for.
*Fix:* "We forget Him for the same reason the farmer forgot his king. All that time, He has been keeping us safe."

**23.** "Part of the reason we forget Him is the world we grew up in." / "Most of us live in democracies, where power starts with the people."
*Problem:* "Part of the reason… is" is an abstract subject, written prose. This is the fourth "most of us", 15 seconds after the third.
*Fix:* "We also forget Him because of the world we grew up in. In most of our countries, power starts with the people."

**24.** ""The earth is the LORD's, and everything in it," says the psalm, "the world, and all who live in it.""
*Problem:* written for the eye. The listener cannot see quotation marks, so after "says the psalm" the second half sounds like the narrator talking.
*Fix:* "As the psalm says, "The earth is the LORD's, and everything in it, the world, and all who live in it." All who live in it." Keep "The Bible was written in the farmer's world, and nobody ever voted God in." It is one of the best lines in the script.

## 6. The king's face

**25.** "When an Assyrian king brought a far-off land under his rule, he had his own likeness carved in stone."
*Problem:* this is the first "likeness" (with "his carved likeness" and "carved their likeness in stone" to come). The refrain word is *image*, and this is where the listener should start hearing it about kings.
*Fix:* "When an Assyrian king brought a far-off land under his rule, he had his own image carved in stone." Keep "He sent his face."

**26.** "It still carries his carved likeness." / "The king himself was far away, and the people of that island knew what his image meant."
*Problem:* the first sentence is decorative and repeats "carved", "likeness" and "still". The second gives a third "far" in 20 seconds (far-off, far across the sea, far away), and "and" should be "but".
*Fix:* cut the first. Then: "The king never set foot on that island, but the people there knew what his image meant."

**27.** "They paid their taxes to Caesar, and in the temple they worshipped the God their psalms called "the great King over all the earth.""
*Problem:* breath (24 words) and a knotted clause. "The God their psalms called" makes the listener work out where the clause starts and ends.
*Fix:* "They paid their taxes to Caesar. And in the temple they sang that God is "the great King over all the earth.""

## 7. The first page

**28.** "Now go back to the first page of the Bible, the page every man in those courts had learned as a boy."
*Problem:* this is the second "Now" hinge, and the sentence runs to 21 words on an appositive tail. "Every man in those courts" comes back almost word for word less than a minute later in b02 ("Every man in those courts knew the answer from the first page of his Scriptures"), and the second time it sounds like filler.
*Fix:* "Go back with me to the first page of the Bible. Every man in those courts had learned it as a boy." Keep "God speaks, the way a king speaks, and what He says simply happens."

**29.** "And then, last of all, He does something every person in that ancient world would have understood."
*Problem:* a teaser hinge, close to "here's where it gets interesting". It promises meaning instead of giving it. Two commas come before the verb, and "would have understood" holds the moment at arm's length.
*Fix:* "Last of all, He does what the kings of that world did." Then "He places His image in His land." The listener hears the parallel without being told it is coming.

**30.** ""Then God said, 'Let us make mankind in our image, in our likeness, so that they may rule…' So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them.""
*Problem:* written for the eye. The ellipsis cannot be heard, so the listener hears "…so that they may rule. So God created…" as one run, with "so… So" glued together. That is 33 words of scripture with no breath, and the second verse sounds like the narrator talking.
*Fix:* "Then God said, "Let us make mankind in our image, in our likeness." Then the Bible says it again, so no one can miss it. "So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them."" If the author wants "rule", say it after the quote in his own words: "His image, set in His land to rule it for Him."

**31.** "Every man in those courts knew the answer from the first page of his Scriptures."
*Problem:* it repeats the line in note 28 ("every man in those courts", "the first page"). "Every man… his Scriptures" is awkward to say. The question before it is put to *you*, but this answer is about *them*, so the pronoun swings.
*Fix:* "So whose face do you carry? The men in those courts had known the answer since they were boys." Keep "Caesar could stamp his face on silver. God had stamped His on them." exactly.

**32.** "The kings of that world carved their likeness in stone and stamped it on metal. God's image is alive."
*Problem:* this is the third "likeness". "God's image is alive" explains the metaphor before it lands, and the metaphor does that work itself. It also starts a run of four neat landings in 40 seconds: silver/them, alive, statue/coin and small/total.
*Fix:* "The kings of that world carved their image in stone and stamped it on metal. You are the statue that breathes, and the coin that walks." Stone pairs with statue and metal with coin, and the line before already says the image is God's.

**33.** "Now hear Jesus' answer again." / "And to God what is God's, the person who carries His."
*Problem:* this is the third "Now" hinge. The sentence ends on a bare "His", which sounds like a dropped word on the ear. Capital letters cannot be heard.
*Fix:* "Listen to Jesus' answer again." This echoes "Listen to the first two lines again" in section 3. Then: "And to God what is God's, the person who carries His image."

**34.** "It means you, every part of you, on every day of the week."
*Problem:* unclear pronoun. "It" could be the answer or "the ownership question". This is also the moment the dramatic question is answered, so the listener should hear its own words come back.
*Fix:* "What belongs to God is you. Every part of you, on every day of the week." Keep "The tax question was small. The ownership question was total." It is the author's line, and with note 32 applied it no longer sits in a pile of couplets.

## 8. What the image gives you

**35.** "This is where your worth comes from." / "It was stamped into you before you had done a single thing." / "A newborn baby carries the King's image in full."
*Problem:* unclear pronouns across a section break the listener cannot see. "This" points at nothing in particular, and "It" grammatically means "worth" when the script means the image. "The King's image" relies on a capital K that cannot be heard, after four minutes of Assyrian kings.
*Fix:* "That image is where your worth comes from. God stamped it on you before you had done a single thing. A newborn baby carries God's image in full." Keep "So does a grandmother who no longer remembers her own name, and so does the prisoner the whole world has written off."

**36.** "The King's image also lays a responsibility on you."
*Problem:* off-voice, a sermon outline. "Lays a responsibility" is an abstract noun phrase, and "also" announces point two of a list. The capital K problem comes back.
*Fix:* "That image asks something of you too."

**37.** "That goes for the image you carry, and for the same image on every face you meet."
*Problem:* the section ends in the abstract. On one hearing, the listener has to work out that "the image you carry" means how you treat yourself.
*Fix:* "So how you treat yourself, and every person you meet, is how you treat Him." This deliberately echoes the line before it.

## 9. The question comes home

**38.** "Now His question was going home with them." / "It had been easy to answer about the coin. It was much harder to answer about themselves."
*Problem:* "His question" is unclear on the ear. The last question the listener heard *them* ask was the tax question, and the capital H cannot be heard. "Answer about" is not quite idiom. This is also the fourth contrast couplet in two minutes.
*Fix:* "They had come to trap a teacher with a coin. They went home carrying the teacher's question, and this time it was about them." Keep "I often think about those men walking home that evening." It is the best transition in the script.

**39.** "Here is one thing to do this week."
*Problem:* a YouTube-style call to action that announces the invitation instead of giving it.
*Fix:* cut it, and open with the action: "This week, every time money passes through your hand, let the face on it ask you that question."

**40.** "Then think of the one part of your life you have been keeping for yourself, and pray over it the words Jesus taught."
*Problem:* breath (23 words) and written syntax. "Pray over it the words" puts the object after the preposition, so the listener stumbles at the most important instruction in the episode. The payoff is also missing. Section 3 said we pray this "quickly, on the way to daily bread", but the ear never hears the word that answers it.
*Fix:* "Then think of the one part of your life you have kept back for yourself. Bring it to God, and pray the words Jesus taught, slowly this time. "Your kingdom come. Your will be done.""

## 10. Next time

**41.** "Next time, we will open the kind of document a great king used to bind a people to himself, and lay it beside the Bible."
*Problem:* breath (25 words). The listener holds "the kind of document…" for 13 words without knowing what it is.
*Fix:* "Next time, we will open a treaty, the kind a great king made to bind a people to himself. Then we will lay it beside the Bible."

**42.** "Then you will hear God say, "I will be your God and you will be my people," the way the first hearers heard it." / "The next episode is called The Treaty of the Great King."
*Problem:* accidental repetition. "Hear… hearers heard" is three in one line, and "next" is said twice in one short paragraph.
*Fix:* "And you will hear God say, "I will be your God and you will be my people," the way it first sounded to Israel. It is called The Treaty of the Great King."

## Closing (channel outro)

**43.** "Leave a comment below, or send Carl a message directly at afaithdrivenlife.com."
*Problem:* off-voice if Carl reads it. An author naming himself in the third person breaks the "audiobook read by its author" feel.
*Fix (if Carl narrates):* "Leave a comment below, or send me a message at afaithdrivenlife.com." This is fixed channel copy, so it is the author's call.

## The 5 best sentences for the ear (keep them)

1. **"The whole trap was small enough to fit in the palm of a hand."** This is the author's own line. It is concrete and ends on the picture. With note 6 applied, it is the first time the listener hears "hand".
2. **"If He said no, He was calling for revolt, and those soldiers were watching."** It gives the stakes in plain words and ends on a threat the listener can see.
3. **"We usually say it quickly, on the way to daily bread."** It is wry, true and in his voice. It catches the listener in the act and sets up the ending.
4. **"That year he brings in the harvest alone."** One picture in eight words, and the feeling does the rest.
5. **"Hold out your hand for a moment, and look at it."** It turns listening into something the listener does, which makes it the best invitation in the episode.

Close behind, also keep: "He sent his face." / "He could forget the king only because the king was doing his job." / "Caesar could stamp his face on silver. God had stamped His on them." / "The Bible was written in the farmer's world, and nobody ever voted God in." / "I often think about those men walking home that evening."

## The 5 worst sentences for the ear (fix first)

1. **"In one small piece of silver, a man claimed to be the son of a god, and the one who stood between his people and heaven."** At 26 words with the subject arriving late, the listener loses the thread at "and the one who" (note 9).
2. **"Then God said, 'Let us make mankind in our image, in our likeness, so that they may rule…' So God created mankind…"** The ellipsis cannot be heard. Two verses run together as 33 words with no breath, and the second sounds like narration (note 30).
3. **"We take His patience to mean that He is absent."** An AI-style aphorism with essay syntax. It brings in a new idea at the exact moment the farmer parallel should land (note 22).
4. **"The King's image also lays a responsibility on you."** A sermon-outline sentence. The subject is an abstract noun, "also" announces point two, and the capital K cannot be heard (note 36).
5. **"Then think of the one part of your life you have been keeping for yourself, and pray over it the words Jesus taught."** This is the episode's one instruction, and its syntax is the most tangled in the script. It also drops the "slowly" that pays off section 3 (note 40).
