## 1. The coin

[DRAWING a02 · existing: the temple courts at Passover, crowds, the money changer, a father and son with a lamb]

It was a Tuesday, in the last week of Jesus' life. Jerusalem was full for the Passover, the feast that remembered how God had brought His people out of slavery in Egypt. Pilgrims crowded the courts of the temple, and among them were some men who had come with a question sharpened into a trap.

[DRAWING a03 · existing: Jesus seated, teaching, people listening]

Jesus was there too, as He had been each day that week. He sat teaching, and people had gathered round to listen.

[DRAWING a04 · existing: the Pharisees and a Herodian come to the front; words "Taxes to Caesar?"]

The men made their way to the front. Some of them were Pharisees, who resented Rome's rule. The others were Herodians, men who were comfortable with Roman power. "Teacher," they said, "we know that you are a man of integrity." Then they asked their question. "Is it right to pay the imperial tax to Caesar or not?"

[DRAWING a05 · existing: Roman soldiers below the fortress, the colonnades of the temple behind them]

Caesar was the emperor in Rome. His armies had conquered this land, and his soldiers kept watch over Jerusalem from a fortress at the corner of the temple courts. At festivals they came out and stood along the colonnades, watching the crowd.

[DRAWING a06 · existing: Jesus where the road forks, the angry crowd on one side ("YES"), soldiers and the tower on the other ("NO")]

Every tax the people paid reminded them who ruled them. To many of them, paying it felt like bowing to a foreign king in God's own land. So everyone listening could see the trap. If Jesus said yes, He would lose the crowd. If He said no, He was calling for revolt, and those soldiers were watching.

[DRAWING a07 · existing: a ringed hand puts the coin into Jesus' open palm]

Jesus knew their hypocrisy. "Why are you trying to trap me?" He asked. "Bring me a denarius and let me look at it." One of them took a silver denarius from his own purse and put it in Jesus' hand. The whole trap was small enough to fit in the palm of a hand.

[DRAWING a08 · existing: the coin, large: the head of Tiberius, then the words around it]

On one side was a man's face, the emperor Tiberius. Around the face ran a claim. Tiberius Caesar, son of the divine Augustus. Augustus was the emperor before Tiberius, and Rome had declared him a god.

[DRAWING a09 · existing: the other side of the coin, the seated figure and the words PONTIF MAXIM]

On the other side, the coin called Tiberius Pontifex Maximus, the highest priest. In one small piece of silver, a man claimed to be the son of a god, and the one who stood between his people and heaven.

[DRAWING a10 · existing: words "Whose image?", Jesus holding the coin up, words "Give back"]

Jesus held it up. "Whose image is this?" He asked them. "And whose inscription?"

"Caesar's," they replied.

Then Jesus said to them, "Give back to Caesar what is Caesar's and to God what is God's."

[DRAWING a11 · existing: the questioners walking away, amazed]

And they were amazed at Him. They left Him and went away.

## 2. What belongs to God?

[DRAWING b01 · existing: the coin, an arrow, Caesar with his hand held out]

The first half of His answer was simple. The coin carried Caesar's face, so it belonged to Caesar. Let him have it back.

[DRAWING p01 · new: beside Caesar's coin, an empty coin with no face, a large question mark, and the words "and to God what is God's?"]

But Jesus had not stopped at Caesar. "And to God what is God's." If the coin with Caesar's face belonged to Caesar, what belonged to God?

[DRAWING p02 · new: the words "What a Kingdom Actually Was" written like a chapter heading, a small crown above them]

The men who heard Him knew the answer, and I believe that is what amazed them. Most of us miss it, because we have forgotten what they knew. They knew what a kingdom actually was.

---

## 3. A word that lost its weight

[DRAWING c01 · new: Jesus standing on the shore of the Sea of Galilee, fishermen and villagers around Him; words "The kingdom of God has come near"]

When Jesus first began to preach in Galilee, this is what He said. "The time has come. The kingdom of God has come near. Repent and believe the good news!" Nobody in that crowd needed the word kingdom explained. They were living inside one, under Rome.

[DRAWING c02 · new: a storybook castle on a hill, a king on a throne, a small dragon far off]

For most of us, the word has gone soft. We hear kingdom and picture a castle on a hill, a king on a throne, perhaps a dragon in the distance. It sounds pleasant, and harmless.

[DRAWING c03 · new: a man and a woman in a church pew, heads bowed; words "Your kingdom come, your will be done"]

Many of us pray for it every Sunday. "Your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven." We usually say it quickly, on the way to daily bread.

[DRAWING c04 · new, added to the same board: an equals sign joining the two halves of the prayer, and under them "where the king's will is done"]

Listen to the first two lines again. "Your kingdom come." "Your will be done." They ask for the same thing twice. A kingdom was the place where the king's will was done. For the people in those temple courts, that was simply how the world worked.

## 4. The edge of a kingdom

[DRAWING d01 · new: a road through open country; beside it an upright stone carved with a king's figure and writing; a traveller with a donkey coming towards it]

In the world of the Bible, a king's land had edges, and stones marked them. Some of those stones survive today, still carrying the names of the kings who set them up. A traveller on an ancient road comes to one of them.

[DRAWING d02 · new: the traveller has passed the stone; ahead of him fields and a village, a tax official at a table, soldiers on a far wall; words "the king's law"]

Everyone who passed a stone like that knew what it meant. From here on, this king's law runs. His officials collect the taxes here, and his soldiers keep the peace. His protection covers everyone on this side of the line. One step past that stone, and the traveller is under a different king.

## 5. The farmer who forgot his king

[DRAWING e01 · new: before dawn, an Assyrian farmer leads an ox out to his field; a mud-brick house behind him]

Now imagine a farmer who lives inside that line, in the ancient kingdom of Assyria. He is up before dawn, working a field he thinks of as his own. His father farmed it before him. He sells his grain and raises his sons. When his father dies, he buries him in that ground.

[DRAWING e02 · new: the farmer resting under a tree at midday; far off on the horizon, a tiny palace]

He has never seen the king, and he has never been near the palace. Whole years go by without the king crossing his mind. As far as he can tell, he is his own man.

[DRAWING e03 · new: at the threshing floor, the king's officials and a scribe measure out the grain and load sacks onto a donkey; the farmer stands by]

Then one harvest, the king's officials arrive. A share of everything he has grown belongs to the throne, and they have come to take it. There is no arguing with them. Nobody asks the farmer whether he agrees.

[DRAWING e04 · new: the farmer's two sons walk away with an overseer towards a great wall being built; the farmer and his wife watch from the door]

Another year, the king is building a great wall, and every household owes him labour. The farmer's sons are called away to work on it, and they go. That year he brings in the harvest alone.

[DRAWING e05 · new: on a far ridge, the spears of an enemy army; between the ridge and the farm, ranks of the king's soldiers; in front, the farmer's family by their house]

Then one spring an enemy army gathers at the border. Now the farmer understands why he has paid and laboured all these years. The king's soldiers are all that stand between that army and his family.

[DRAWING e06 · new: the field and the house, safe behind the line of soldiers; the farmer looking out at them; words "held under the king"]

His field was held under the king the whole time. He could forget the king only because the king was doing his job.

[DRAWING e07 · new: a modern morning, a parent heading out of the door with a bag, children at the breakfast table, a full calendar on the wall]

Most of us live a lot like that farmer. We get up, go to work, raise our children and run our own lives. Whole seasons can go by without God crossing our minds. We take His patience to mean that He is absent.

[DRAWING e08 · new: two halves; on the left a ballot box with an arrow rising to a parliament; on the right a throne with an arrow coming down to the people]

Part of the reason we forget Him is the world we grew up in. Most of us live in democracies, where power starts with the people. We vote our leaders in, and we can vote them out. I am grateful for that. In the farmer's world, power ran the other way. It came down from the throne.

[DRAWING e09 · new: hills, a sea, a village and its people, all under one wide sky; words "The earth is the LORD's"]

The Bible was written in the farmer's world, and nobody ever voted God in. "The earth is the LORD's, and everything in it," says the psalm, "the world, and all who live in it." All who live in it. That includes the farmer, and it includes you.

## 6. The king's face

[DRAWING f01 · new: the gate of a city far from Assyria; beside it a tall stone carved with the figure of an Assyrian king; townspeople passing beneath it]

That farmer never saw his king. So how did a king make his claim felt in places he would never visit? He sent his face. When an Assyrian king brought a far-off land under his rule, he had his own likeness carved in stone. He set it up where the people of that land would pass it day after day.

[DRAWING f02 · new: close on the carved king's face, looking out over the roofs of the city; words "This place is mine."]

One of those stones was found on the island of Cyprus, far across the sea from the king's palace. It still carries his carved likeness. The king himself was far away, and the people of that island knew what his image meant. This place was his now, and so were the people in it.

[DRAWING f03 · new: a market stall; hands passing coins; one coin drawn large with a king's head on it]

Later, rulers found a way to send their face even further. They stamped it on coins, and the king's face went into every market in the land.

[DRAWING f04 · new: the temple courts drawn small; in front, a hand holding Caesar's coin; above, the words "the great King over all the earth"]

Remember that coin in the temple courts? Caesar's face had travelled across his empire into the courts of God's house. The men who carried that coin lived in two kingdoms at once. They paid their taxes to Caesar, and in the temple they worshipped the God their psalms called "the great King over all the earth."

## 7. The first page

[DRAWING g01 · new: darkness over deep water, light breaking across it; words "Let there be light"]

Now go back to the first page of the Bible, the page every man in those courts had learned as a boy. God speaks, the way a king speaks, and what He says simply happens. "And God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light."

[DRAWING g02 · new: dry land rising from the sea, trees and plants, birds in the sky, animals on the ground]

Day by day He fills His world. Land rises out of the waters. Plants cover it, and the sky and the seas fill with living things. All of it is His, because He made it.

[DRAWING g03 · new: a man and a woman standing in the garden, light falling on them; words "in our image"]

And then, last of all, He does something every person in that ancient world would have understood. He places His image in His land. "Then God said, 'Let us make mankind in our image, in our likeness, so that they may rule…' So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them."

[DRAWING b02 · existing, moved here from 1:44: words "Whose face do you carry?", the coin with a human face (IMAGO DEI) and the scroll, words "in the image of God"]

So whose face do you carry? Every man in those courts knew the answer from the first page of his Scriptures. Caesar could stamp his face on silver. God had stamped His on them.

[DRAWING g04 · new: on the left a stone statue of a king on its plinth; on the right a living man and woman walking out into the world]

The kings of that world carved their likeness in stone and stamped it on metal. God's image is alive. You are the statue that breathes, and the coin that walks.

[DRAWING b03 · existing, moved here from 1:55: Caesar's coin, arrow, "Caesar"; a person, arrow, "God"; the days of the week, every one ticked]

Now hear Jesus' answer again. Give back to Caesar what is Caesar's, the coin with his face on it. And to God what is God's, the person who carries His. The tax question was small. The ownership question was total. It means you, every part of you, on every day of the week.

## 8. What the image gives you

[DRAWING h01 · new: three people side by side; a mother holding a newborn; an old woman in a chair, a hand holding hers; a prisoner sitting in a cell]

This is where your worth comes from. It was stamped into you before you had done a single thing. A newborn baby carries the King's image in full. So does a grandmother who no longer remembers her own name, and so does the prisoner the whole world has written off.

[DRAWING h02 · new: one person helping another to his feet, face to face]

The King's image also lays a responsibility on you. In the ancient world, how you treated a king's image was how you treated the king himself. That goes for the image you carry, and for the same image on every face you meet.

---

## 9. The question comes home

[DRAWING i01 · new: evening in a narrow Jerusalem street; two of the questioners walking home, one looking down at the coin in his palm]

I often think about those men walking home that evening. They had come to trap a teacher with a coin. Now His question was going home with them. It had been easy to answer about the coin. It was much harder to answer about themselves.

[DRAWING i02 · new: an open hand, palm up, drawn large; words "Whose image is this?"]

And now that question has come all the way to you. Hold out your hand for a moment, and look at it. Whose image is this?

[DRAWING j01 · new: a hand paying at a shop counter; beside it, two open hands offering up a small box; words "Your kingdom come. Your will be done."]

Here is one thing to do this week. Every time money passes through your hand, let the face on it ask you that question. Then think of the one part of your life you have been keeping for yourself, and pray over it the words Jesus taught. "Your kingdom come. Your will be done."
