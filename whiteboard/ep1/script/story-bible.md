## The one meaning

> **The King's image is stamped on you, so all of you belongs to Him, every part and every day. That is where your worth comes from, and your calling.**

## The spine

**The coin in the temple courts** (Mark 12:13–17, with Luke 20:26) is the frame. Inside it runs **one story, the farmer's**, and the teaching of the published Episode 1 is told through it.

1. **What they knew.** The word kingdom has gone soft for us. In their world a boundary stone marked a king's land, and behind it stood a king, his land and his people, held together by his word.
2. **The farmer, in Assyria.** He works a field he thinks of as his own. At its edge stands a stone carved with the face of a king he has never seen. The king's men count his grain and his sons. When an enemy army gathers beyond the stone, his family shelters behind the wall his sons built, and the king's soldiers stand on it. The field was never his alone.
3. **The Bible's King.** The Bible was written in that world: "I will be your God and you will be My people"; "The land is Mine"; the King of kings; the Great King who rescued Israel.
4. **The four ways the king's will reached the farmer's village:**
   - the decree, carried by the officials who came for his grain;
   - the herald who called his sons to the wall;
   - the written covenant read aloud;
   - the king's image on the stone.

   Each is matched in the Bible: Sinai, the prophets and Nathan, the law read at the Festival of Tabernacles, and the image. Later kings stamped their faces on coins, and that brings us back to Caesar's coin in God's house.
5. **The first page.** God places His own image in His land. The farmer carried a greater King's image all his life without knowing it, and so do you.
6. **What the image gives.** The farmer's king counted what he could hand over; God's image was on us before we handed over anything. The image is handled with care.
7. **The road home.** The man who brought the coin looks at the hand that holds it. Then the listener holds out theirs.

## Guardrail: the king must not make God feel harsh

The test listener flinched at the king who takes grain and sons. In Scripture that list is a warning about human kings (1 Samuel 8:11–17), and Assyria is the great oppressor (Isaiah 10:5–14). So the script does three things.

1. **Carries over to God only ownership and protection.**
   - Ownership: "The field was never his alone"; "the king's land" (e06); "all of it belongs to Him, because He made every part of it."
   - Protection: the wall the sons built is the wall that saves the family; "All that time God has been keeping watch over us, as surely as those soldiers stood on the wall."
   - The taking stays with the human king.
2. **Plants worth by contrast.** The officials *count* the grain and *count* the sons (e03–e04). At h01 that counting is turned over: "The farmer's king counted what the farmer could hand over… God's image was on you before you had handed Him anything at all."
3. **Names Assyria in v6, as the published episode and Carl's book do.**
   - v5 had left it unnamed after a test listener's "wait, them?" moment.
   - Carl's rule says nothing from the published episode is cut, and his book says "Imagine a farmer in the ancient kingdom of, say, Assyria".
   - Points 1 and 2 carry the guardrail now. The v6 first-time listener still asked "good king or bad?", so this is on Carl's decision list.

## For the video description (not narrated)

Museum and find-spot facts belong here, not in the story.

- **The coin.** The silver denarius of Tiberius, the "tribute penny", was struck at Lugdunum (Lyon) between AD 15 and 37. Obverse: TI CAESAR DIVI AVG F AVGVSTVS, "Tiberius Caesar Augustus, son of the divine Augustus". Reverse: PONTIF MAXIM, "highest priest".
- **Soldiers at the festivals.** Josephus describes the Roman garrison of the Antonia fortress spreading along the temple porticoes at every festival "to watch the people" (*Jewish War* 5.244).
- **A king's face in a far land.** Sargon II's basalt stele, about 2 m tall, was set up at Kition (modern Larnaca, Cyprus) around 707 BC. It was found in 1845 and is now in Berlin. Please check before saying it is on display; the accuracy editor believes it has been off view since October 2023.
- **A king's border stone.** Adad-nirari III's Pazarcık stele (805 BC) fixed the border between two kingdoms. It was found near Kahramanmaraş, Turkey.
- **The farmer** is a composite drawn from Assyrian records of taxes, labour and military service (Postgate, 1974). He stands for many ordinary people under ancient kings.
- **Scripture notice.** "Scripture quotations are from the Holy Bible, New International Version®, NIV®. Copyright © 1973, 1978, 1984, 2011 by Biblica, Inc.™ Used by permission. All rights reserved worldwide." The episode quotes well under the 500 verses the NIV allows with a notice.

- **"Jesus plus nothing."** Carl's sign-off. A one-line explanation here may help new viewers (decision 5 below).

## What changed from v3 to v4, and why
