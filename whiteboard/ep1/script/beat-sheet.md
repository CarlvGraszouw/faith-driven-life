**Timing rule** (story-script skill): a drawing needs its draw time plus about 1.5 s to breathe. At 2.5 spoken words a second, a beat needs at least `(draw s + 1.5) × 2.5` words. Every story beat meets it. Where a beat has more words than its minimum, the drawing finishes and holds (slow push-in) while the story goes on.

**Draw times.**
- **Existing art:** the natural times from `node render.js ../preview/ep1-2min.json --plan`, summing every part and on-screen word of the scene.
- **New art:** estimates by type. Wide and crowd scenes 10–12 s, full scenes with two to four figures 7–9 s, simpler scenes 5–7 s, coins 3–5 s, and handwritten words about 0.12 s a character.
- Artists should keep new scenes inside these budgets, or the narration for that beat must grow.

**Recording pace matters as much as the words.** The v1 recording ran at about 3.4 words a second, pauses included. At that pace every beat gets about a quarter less time than this sheet allows, which is why the two-minute preview had to squeeze the redrawn temple to 66% of its natural drawing time. Record at audiobook pace, about 150 words a minute (2.5 a second). If the faster voice has to stay, every "min words" figure needs to rise by about 35%.

## Notes for the timeline

- **Multi-part scenes:** set each part's `at` to the words it illustrates (given in the drawing column for a04, a06, a08, a10, b02 and b03). Write on-screen words as the narrator says them, not before.
- **Shared boards:** p01 sits beside b01's coin, and f05 repeats that pair. c04 is added to c03's board, e02b to e02's, and g02 to g01's.
- **a10b reuses a06.** Draw the same fork and the same people a moment later, with the anger gone.
- **e01–e06 are one family, one farm and one stone.** Keep the farmer, his wife, the two sons, the house, the field and the stone the same in every drawing. From e05 on, the town wall is the one the sons were building in e04.
- **The king is never drawn as a person.** He appears only as the face carved on the stone, which keeps the king remote, as the story needs.
- **The stone (e01, e02, e02b, e05, e06, g04):** a tall round-topped stele, the king carved in profile in a long fringed robe and tall royal headdress, right hand raised, symbols of his gods above his head, lines of cuneiform below. Model it on Assyrian royal and border stelae such as Sargon II's stele from Kition or Adad-nirari III's Pazarcık stele. The narration does not name Assyria; the art can keep the Assyrian style.
- **Dress:** farmers wear knee-length belted tunics and sandals, with full beards. Officials and scribes wear long fringed robes; the scribe holds a clay tablet and stylus. Soldiers wear pointed helmets and carry spears and round shields.
- **g03 does not draw God.** The man and the woman stand in light, and the words "in our image" carry the moment.
- **i01 → i02 is the peak.** Give i02 its full hold, and do not start j01's drawing until the question "Whose image is this?" has been asked and has rested.
- **If time must be cut,** the beat that can lose a sentence with least damage is c02 (the castle). Do not cut a10b, e02–e06, f05, i01 or i02.
