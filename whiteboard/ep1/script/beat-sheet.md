# Beat sheet — Episode 1: What a Kingdom Actually Was (v3)

One row per drawing, in narration order. It goes with `ep1-script-v3.md`: each `[DRAWING id]` cue there is a row here, and the word counts below are taken from the script itself.

**Timing rule** (story-script skill): a drawing needs its draw time plus about 1.5 s to breathe. At 2.5 spoken words a second, a beat needs at least `(draw s + 1.5) × 2.5` words. Every beat meets it. Where a beat has more words than its minimum, the drawing finishes and holds (slow push-in) while the story goes on.

**Draw times.** For existing art, these are the natural times from `node render.js ../preview/ep1-2min.json --plan`, summing every part and on-screen word of the scene. New art times are estimates by type: wide and crowd scenes 10–12 s, full scenes with two to four figures 7–9 s, simpler scenes 5–7 s, coins 3–5 s, and handwritten words about 0.12 s a character. Artists should keep new scenes inside these budgets, or the narration for that beat must grow.

**Recording pace matters as much as the words.** The v1 recording ran at about 3.4 words a second, pauses included (2,073 words over 615 s). At that pace every beat gets about a quarter less time than this sheet allows. With the v1 audio timing, the two-minute preview had to squeeze the redrawn temple to 66% of its natural drawing time and six other scenes to 71–93%. Record at audiobook pace, about 150 words a minute (2.5 a second). If the faster voice has to stay, every "min words" figure needs to rise by about 35%.

**Moved, not redrawn.** b02 ("Whose face do you carry?" with the IMAGO DEI coin) and b03 (Caesar / God, the week ticked) move from 1:44–2:07 to the climax in section 7, where they answer the question. The new p01 (an empty coin and a question mark beside Caesar's coin) sets them up. a01–b01 keep their order and art; only their narration and timing change.

**Totals:** 46 drawings (15 existing, 31 new). The story narration is 1,848 words, 12:19 at 2.5 words a second; with the unchanged intro and outro the episode runs about 12:48.

## Notes for the timeline

- **Multi-part scenes:** set each part's `at` to the words it illustrates (given in the drawing column for a04, a06, a08, a10, b02, b03 and c04). Write on-screen words as the narrator says them, not before.
- **p01 and b01 share a board.** Draw p01's empty coin to the left of Caesar's coin so the pair reads "Caesar's coin → Caesar; empty coin → ?". b03 later shows the completed pair.
- **c03 and c04 share a board.** c04 only adds the equals sign and one line of words.
- **e01–e06 are one family and one farm.** Keep the farmer, his wife, the two sons, the house and the field the same in every drawing, so the viewer follows one man through one story.
- **f01, f02 and d01 stones:** an Assyrian royal stele, a tall round-topped stone with the king in profile (long fringed robe, royal headdress, raised right hand) and divine symbols above his head, like Sargon II's stele from Kition or Adad-nirari III's border stele from Pazarcık. Draw no Babylonian kudurru (those were kept in temples, not at roadsides).
- **Assyrian dress (e01–e06):** farmers in knee-length belted tunics and sandals, full beards; officials and scribes in long fringed robes; soldiers in pointed helmets with spears and round shields.
- **g03 does not draw God.** The man and the woman stand in light; the words "in our image" carry the moment.
- **If time must be cut,** the beats that can lose a sentence with least damage are f02 (the Cyprus sentence), c02 (the castle) and e08 (the democracy contrast). Do not cut e03–e06 (the farmer's turn), p01 (the question) or i02 (the ending).
